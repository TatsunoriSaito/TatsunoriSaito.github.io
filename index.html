01

About

I am a Ph.D. student at Keio University, where my research focuses on radar sensing for human-centric applications. My work explores how millimeter-wave (mmWave) radar can capture subtle physiological motion — such as respiration and heartbeat — from a distance, without cameras or wearable devices.

I am particularly interested in the intersection of classical radar signal processing and modern machine learning: designing models that are robust to body movement, multipath, and multi-person environments, while remaining interpretable and efficient enough for real-time embedded deployment.

I am a member of the Ohtsuki Lab at Keio University. Before that, I completed my undergraduate and master's studies at Nihon University in the Toda Lab, where I worked on mmWave radar–based vital-sign and human sensing. I am always happy to discuss collaborations on contactless sensing, healthcare monitoring, and wireless human sensing.

02

Research

My research aims to turn radio signals into reliable measurements of human state. Below are the main directions I am currently pursuing.

Vital Signs

Contactless Vital-Sign Monitoring

Estimating respiration and heart rate from the chest-wall displacement captured by mmWave radar, with robust phase extraction and harmonic suppression for accurate beat-to-beat measurement.

  • FMCW radar
  • Phase unwrapping
  • HRV

Human Sensing

mmWave Human Sensing

Detecting, localizing, and tracking people from sparse radar point clouds and range–Doppler–angle maps, enabling privacy-preserving presence, posture, and activity recognition indoors.

  • MIMO radar
  • Point clouds
  • Tracking

Machine Learning

Learning-Based Radar Signal Processing

Designing deep-learning models that operate directly on radar signals, improving robustness to motion artifacts, clutter, and multi-person interference while staying light enough for edge devices.

  • Deep learning
  • Denoising
  • Edge AI

Applications

Healthcare & Daily-Life Monitoring

Bringing radar sensing into real environments — sleep monitoring, elderly care, and in-vehicle occupant sensing — and validating against clinical reference devices in long-term studies.

  • Sleep
  • Elderly care
  • In-cabin sensing
